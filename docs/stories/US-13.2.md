## Story

As a property director, I want a single-page summary of the portfolio's financial health so that I can judge the year at a glance without opening any sub-reports.

## What was done

1. **Four KPI cards** placed at the top of the page: Total Billed (PKR), Collection Rate (D17 cohort, %), Occupancy % and Arrears Ratio.  Each card uses its matching measure from `docs/powerbi/measures.dax`; formatting is PKR for money measures and two decimal places for percentages to match the analytical queries.
2. **Monthly trend visual** — a line/column combo using `vw_monthly_collections` from `lue_reporting`: columns for Billed and Collected, a line for Collection Rate, axis set to `month_label`, tooltips show Arrears.  The LAG-based year-ago line uses `Cash Received LY` (already in measures.dax).
3. **Arrears bar chart** — horizontal bars from `mart_collections_monthly`, sorted descending by Arrears, top 10 buildings, so the director sees the worst performers immediately.
4. **Slicers** — Fiscal Year (`fy_label` from `vw_dim_date`), City (`city` from `vw_dim_building`), Building Class (`building_class` from `vw_dim_building`).  All three are connected to every visual on the page; city and building-class slicers are single-select for a clean reading.
5. **Layout** — Lahore Urban Estates navy theme (`lue_theme.json`), 1280 × 720 canvas, grid snap on; KPI cards top row, trend visual bottom-left (60 % width), arrears chart bottom-right (38 % width).  Page name in Power BI: **"Executive Overview"**.
6. **Build guide reference** — steps 8–10 of `docs/powerbi/README.md` cover creating the page, adding visuals, and setting slicer sync; this page follows those steps exactly.

## Decisions

- KPI cards show the **full-window** value (no slicer on year for the headline) with a subtitle showing the fiscal year from the slicer — this matches the Q2/Q3 arrears reading in the analytical queries.
- The trend visual starts at FY2023-24 (the earliest full fiscal year in the data window) to avoid partial-year distortion.
- Arrears bar shows the absolute PKR arrears per building (not the ratio) so the director sees the largest absolute exposure rather than the worst percentage, which a small building would always win.
