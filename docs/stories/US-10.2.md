## Story

As a dashboard builder, I want pre-aggregated monthly marts, so that reports load quickly and consistently.

## What was done

1. **Four materialized views**, each grain a unique index:

   | Mart | Grain | Rows (full profile) |
   |---|---|---|
   | `mart_collections_monthly` | month x building | 1,440 |
   | `mart_occupancy_monthly` | month x building x unit type | 2,880 |
   | `mart_maintenance_monthly` | raised month x building x unit type x category version x vendor version | 5,637 |
   | `mart_owner_portfolio_monthly` | month x owner | 31,424 |

   Collections hold the cohort basis (D17) and cash received side by side, and arrears at each month end (the rule
   of the occupancy fact: lines past due minus the payments made against them by then, computed as a running total).
   The owner mart holds rent collected, maintenance cost, the fee (an 8 percent assumption, in
   `vw_reporting_parameter`) and NOI (Q12).
2. **Additivity respected**: every mart stores sums and counts only, so a rate is a ratio of sums at any level.
   Occupancy is occupied unit-days over unit-days, the average of daily occupied units over units, never a sum of
   days; month-end values (rent roll, arrears, units, active leases) are semi-additive and documented as such.
3. **Refresh**: `lue_reporting.refresh_marts()` refreshes all four, concurrently once they hold data (readers keep
   the previous rows); the pipeline calls it in its new last stage, `marts` (`etl/reporting.py`, step
   `refresh_marts` in the run log, with the marts' lineage). About 6 seconds on the full profile.

Found on the way: a first version of the collections mart took 23 seconds, because PostgreSQL re-read the facts
for every row of its arrears subquery; a running total brought it to under 1 second. And the test caught the
maintenance mart truncating `days_to_resolve` (NUMERIC(6,2), days with hours) to whole days; it keeps the decimals.
