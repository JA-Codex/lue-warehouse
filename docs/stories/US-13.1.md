## Story

As a dashboard builder, I want a clean model with consistent measures, so that every visual calculates the same way.

## What was done

1. **The model** ([`docs/powerbi/model.json`](../powerbi/model.json)): 15 tables from `lue_reporting`, loaded as
   `lue_reader` in Import mode: 7 dimensions (date, building, unit type, vendor, maintenance category, owner, and
   the tenant analysis as the tenant dimension), 5 facts (the four marts and the tenants by building), two hidden
   reference views and the data window. 17 relationships, many to one, single direction, each from a dimension to a
   fact, so every filter path is one step and none is ambiguous. The date table is `vw_dim_date` without its
   special rows (the 1900 dates), marked on `full_date`; a month key is the date key of the month's first day.
2. **Two views added to the reporting layer** (`sql/50_reporting/01_reporting.sql`, documented in
   `docs/reporting.md`): `vw_dim_unit_type`, so one slicer filters the occupancy and maintenance marts together, and
   `vw_tenant_by_building` for the drill-through (US-13.3).
3. **The measures** ([`docs/powerbi/measures.dax`](../powerbi/measures.dax)): 51, pasted in one go in DAX query
   view: Billed, Collected (Cohort), Collection Rate (D17), Cash Received with the year before, Arrears, Arrears
   Ratio, Occupancy %, Active Leases, Average Days to Resolve, SLA Breach % and the rest, formatted in PKR and
   percentages. Every rate is a ratio of sums; month-end values (arrears, active leases, units, rent roll) are taken
   at the last month of the selection; the portfolio measures drop the building and vendor filters for comparison.
4. **The theme** ([`lue_theme.json`](../powerbi/lue_theme.json)) and the connection parameters in the build guide
   ([`docs/powerbi/README.md`](../powerbi/README.md), steps 1 to 7 and 13), so the PBIX opens and refreshes on
   another machine.
5. **The SQL side of every measure**: `python scripts/powerbi_expected.py` evaluates each measure from the same
   marts the way the DAX does, as `lue_reader`; `scripts/test_powerbi.py` checks those values against queries on the
   facts; `docs/powerbi/validation.dax` (generated from the same list) shows the same table inside Power BI.

The arrears ratio is the arrears at the end of the selection over the amount billed from the window start up to
then: over the whole window it is Q2's ratio (GLB03 21.8 percent), and for one month it stays a share of what was
billed (a month's billing alone would give ratios above 100 percent).
