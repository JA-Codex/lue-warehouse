## Story

As a performance tester, I want a repeatable way to measure queries, so that before and after numbers are comparable.

## What was done

1. **Queries**: Q1 (collection rate by building) and Q7 (vacancy spells) exactly as they stand in
   `sql/60_analytics/analytical.sql`, and a large occupancy aggregate (one quarter by city, B4); with them the
   date-bounded and selective shapes a dashboard sends (Q1 for one month, one unit over a year, the current tenant
   list), in `sql/70_performance/01_benchmark_queries.sql`.
2. **Unpartitioned copy**: `lue_dw.bench_occupancy_unpartitioned`, the same rows and primary key in one table, made
   by the harness and dropped after it.
3. **Harness**: `python scripts/benchmark.py`, one command. `VACUUM ANALYZE`, a warm-up of every query, then
   interleaved rounds: in each, every query is measured at the baseline, the candidate indexes are created, every
   query is measured again. A measurement is the fastest of 3 back-to-back `EXPLAIN (ANALYZE, BUFFERS, FORMAT JSON)`
   runs: execution and planning time, total cost, plan type (scans, partitions read, indexes used), buffers, into
   `baseline.csv` and `after.csv`. JIT and parallel workers are off, so serial plans compare access paths.

Found on the way: on the measuring machine (a shared cloud machine) a query of a few milliseconds can take twice as
long from one run to the next. Measuring all of the baseline and then all of the after let a slow spell fall on one
side (Q1 seemed 30 percent faster with an index it did not use). Interleaving the rounds and keeping the fastest of
three back-to-back runs fixed that: Q1 now reads 211 against 224 ms, the same within the noise, as its unchanged plan
says it should.
