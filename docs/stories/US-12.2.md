## Story

As a performance tester, I want to test candidate indexes and partitioning, so that each optimisation is justified by
measurement.

## What was done

1. **Candidate indexes** in `sql/70_performance/02_indexes.sql`, each with its purpose: `(due_date_key, unit_key)` on
   the invoice lines and on the payments, the pack's `(unit_key, snapshot_date_key)` on the occupancy fact (on every
   partition), and a partial index on the current `dim_tenant` rows. `03_drop_indexes.sql` returns to the baseline.
   None is in the deploy (D20).
2. **Measured** before and after (US-12.1's harness): **B2** (Q1 for one month) 22.1 to 4.1 ms, cost 8,837 to 4,750,
   sequential to bitmap scans; **B5** (one unit over a year) 17.5 to 0.97 ms, cost 17,043 to 1,319, partition scans
   to index lookups. Every run after was faster than every run before.
3. **Where it did not help**, each explained: Q1 over the whole window (the planner rightly keeps the sequential
   scans); Q7 (the index is not used); the partial index (chosen, but 0.7 ms on a small dimension); and partitioning
   for the quarter (pruning reads 3 partitions and the cost falls 3.8-fold, but the time does not, because the
   date-first primary key already serves date ranges: partitioning pays in maintenance).
4. **Cost of each index**: size (42.9 MB for the occupancy index, 26 percent of the fact; 2.3 and 2.2 MB for the date
   indexes; 48 kB), build time (median of 9 builds) and load cost (each fact's slice re-inserted without and with its
   index, rolled back: +6 to +13 percent, within the variation; the foreign-key checks dominate).

`scripts/test_facts.py` accepts the four documented indexes (after `benchmark.py --keep`) and nothing else.
